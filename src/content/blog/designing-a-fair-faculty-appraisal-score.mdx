## Why Appraisal Scores Lose Trust

Few processes in a university attract as much quiet frustration as annual appraisal. Faculty fill long self-assessment forms, attach piles of certificates, and receive a number with little explanation. Colleagues compare notes and find the same activity scored differently in different departments.

The problem is rarely the idea of a score. It is how the score is built. Five principles make the difference.

## Principle 1: Score Only Verified Records

An appraisal score should never be based on self-reported claims that nobody has checked. If a record has not been verified against its proof, it should not count yet.

This has a useful side effect. Once faculty know that only verified work counts, they start entering work promptly and attaching proper proof throughout the year, rather than assembling a file at appraisal time.

## Principle 2: Group Work into Clear Categories

A good structure reflects the full range of academic work, not just research output. A typical set of categories might be:

- **Teaching and academic content**: Course delivery, academic content development
- **Research and publications**: Journal and conference papers, books and chapters
- **Projects and intellectual property**: External research projects, seed money projects, consultancy, patents
- **Guidance and mentoring**: Ph.D. guidance, PG and multidisciplinary guidance, student achievements
- **Institutional contribution**: Portfolios and coordinatorships, committee roles, events organised
- **External recognition**: Guest lectures, professional memberships, awards

Each category should be defined clearly enough that two verifiers would put the same record in the same place.

## Principle 3: Agree on Weightage Before the Year Starts

Weightage reflects what the university values, and it should be decided openly by academic leadership, not adjusted after the results are in. Publish the categories, weights and scoring rules at the start of the academic year, and keep them stable for that year.

Different cadres can reasonably have different weightage. An assistant professor in the first years of service and a senior professor leading a research centre contribute in different ways.

## Principle 4: Cap Each Category

Without limits, one very productive category can dominate the score and discourage balanced contribution. A cap on each category keeps the score meaningful:

```
Category score = min(points earned, category cap) x category weight
Appraisal score = sum of all category scores
```

Caps also make the score harder to game by entering many small items of the same kind.

## A Worked Example

Consider a fictional faculty member, Dr. Meera Joshi, in the Department of Electronics & Communication. Assume her university uses a 100-point scale with each category capped at 100 raw points before weighting. With illustrative weights:

- **Teaching and academic content** at 30 percent: 90 raw points gives 27.0
- **Research and publications** at 25 percent: 100 raw points, capped, gives 25.0
- **Projects and intellectual property** at 15 percent: 40 raw points gives 6.0
- **Guidance and mentoring** at 10 percent: 70 raw points gives 7.0
- **Institutional contribution** at 10 percent: 80 raw points gives 8.0
- **External recognition** at 10 percent: 50 raw points gives 5.0

Her appraisal score is 78.0. More importantly, she can see exactly where each point came from, and which verified records produced it.

## Principle 5: Show Every Faculty Member the Breakdown

Transparency turns the score from a verdict into feedback. Each faculty member should be able to see:

1. The verified records counted in each category
2. Any records still Pending or returned for Correction, which do not count yet
3. How the caps and weights were applied

When people can trace their own score, disputes drop sharply, and the conversations that remain are about the rules, which is where they belong.

## Handling Edge Cases

### Co-Authored Work

Decide in advance whether co-authored work is shared or credited in full to each author, and apply the rule consistently.

### Work Spanning Years

Count each record once, in the year it was verified or completed, and say which.

### New Faculty

Consider pro-rating caps for faculty who joined partway through the year.

## Conclusion

A fair appraisal score is not a clever formula. It is verified data, clear categories, weightage agreed in advance, sensible caps and complete transparency. Get those right, and appraisal becomes a conversation about growth rather than a debate about paperwork.
